## Introduction {#sec:intro}

A learner compounds experience when solving earlier problems makes later, related problems cheaper to solve. Learning to learn has a natural in-context form in language models: if a prompt holds several complete instances of a structured task, the model may do better on the next instance than it would without them. Such improvement is often read as evidence that the model has abstracted the shared structure. The reading matters, because an abstraction transfers to instances that share the structure but none of the surface, whereas reuse of the examples does not.

Behavioural improvement alone cannot establish abstraction, because at least three other explanations produce it: context length by itself changes performance; an earlier example may contain the answer, which a model can recall or copy without inference; and a model can answer by analogy to the closest stored example. Exemplar matching improves with more examples just as abstraction does, and the two differ only on types that the history does not contain: a learner that stores the examples and one that has extracted their rules hold the same information about the task, and differ only in what that information makes cheaply usable [@xu2020usable]. A test of compounding therefore has to control context length, separate recall from inference, and include held-out types.

Algebraic operation tables make such a test exact. In a partial Cayley table, whether a missing cell is fixed by the Latin property alone, by the loop axioms (identity and cancellation), or only through associativity can be decided exactly, so each cell receives a *forcing class* that states what a learner must know to answer it. Fresh, arbitrary word labels remove semantic cues, and the earlier tables can be replaced by length-matched tables without group structure, restricted to isomorphism types other than the target's, or made to contain the target's own labelled table. The closest work, In-Context Algebra [@todd2026ica], trains small transformers on sequences of group facts with symbols reassigned in every sequence, but does not vary the number of earlier tables, label cells by forcing class, compare against an exemplar baseline at equal budget, or evaluate pretrained language models; we know of no published evaluation of pretrained language models on in-context group-table completion with fresh labels (@sec:related).

We turn this design into a *compounding fingerprint*. A model sees a **history** of $t = 0$, 1, 2 or 4 complete earlier tables, each in its own fresh single-token English words, then a target table of order $n = 5$, 6 or 8 that reveals $k$ of its cells, the **cue**; every unrevealed cell is queried and labelled with its forcing class, such as Latin-forced (L), loop-forced (O) or associativity-forced (G). Base histories are compared with a length-matched **scramble control**, **held-out types**, a **leak control** that contains the target's own labelled table, and non-associative loops, and a preregistered decision rule maps the resulting signature to the learner classes R, N, N_iso, A and B-type. We applied it to seven exact idealised learners, to nine checkpoints (ten model–precision configurations) from three families [@qwen3; @smollm2; @olmo2], the three largest in 4-bit nf4 quantisation whose preregistered control failed its tolerance, to seven small transformers trained from scratch, and to the released In-Context Algebra model; all model inference and training ran on one 8 GB laptop GPU. Our contributions are:

- **An exact, preregistered measurement, validated on exact learners only.** On identical trials, the decision rule returns the correct class for all seven idealised learners at $n = 6$ and $n = 8$, with 0 misclassifications. At $n = 6$, abstraction and exemplar matching both gain about +0.70 over scramble on G cells, and only held-out transfer separates them cleanly: +0.70 for abstraction against +0.03 for exemplar matching. A post-hoc check against baselines restricted to the held-out target types confirms the separation (@sec:r-ideal). The branch that separates abstraction from exemplar matching has not yet been exercised on a learned system.
- **An upper bound on history benefit in pretrained models up to 8B, on this format.** All ten configurations are class R: none reaches the preregistered Latin-inference threshold ($\mathrm{acc}_L$ 0.14–0.29 at $n = 6$, R threshold 0.417), and in the eight models swept over cue density this holds even at dense cues where one-pass row/column elimination would score 0.92 (post hoc). None gains from four group tables beyond the length-matched scramble: the contrast lies between −0.019 and +0.026, every 95% CI includes 0, and the upper bounds are at most +0.056 (+0.069 with a post-hoc Bonferroni correction across the ten configurations), an order of magnitude below exemplar copying (+0.72) or abstraction (+0.70). Recall is family-dependent: Qwen3 recalls a previously seen labelled table at 0.95–0.96 at 4–8B, OLMo-2 at 0.31–0.37 (@sec:r-llm; @sec:r-cal).
- **A count baseline.** Small transformers trained from scratch on group, Latin and loop diets learn a global count shortcut, answering with the symbol least represented in the cue; the post-hoc count learner C matches five of the six learned networks to within 0.001–0.007. On the in-training evaluation the shortcut alone scores 0.33 at $n = 6$, enough to pass the preregistered learning-failure threshold, so passing that threshold does not show that the task was learned (@sec:r-trained).
- **A positive control on a real network.** The released In-Context Algebra model is classified non-R, non-B: at $t = 0$, Latin- and loop-forced cells score 0.89–0.99 and associativity-forced cells 0.53–0.70. In a post hoc split, it beats an exact loop-level baseline at $n = 6$ and $n = 8$ but not at $n = 5$, and it partly answers by commutative copying. Its class changes during training (exploratory), and its 16-token vocabulary leaves the compounding branch untested (@sec:r-ica).
- **A validity test for activation "mode" probes.** The compute–retrieve (CR) axis of our earlier preprint [@ramdan2026cr] separates its own calibration prompts with AUC 0.96–0.99, but on a format- and length-matched (not vocabulary-matched) retrieval/inference contrast it moves significantly in the predicted direction in 0 of 5 models and is significantly reversed in 3. An exploratory control attributes 64–87% of the reversal to context vocabulary (@app:cr).

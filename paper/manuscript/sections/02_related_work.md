## Related work {#sec:related}

#### In-context learning of algebraic structure

The closest prior work is *In-Context Algebra* [@todd2026ica]. Todd et al. train small transformers (4 layers, 8 heads, width 1024) on sequences of facts $xy = z$ from cyclic groups C3–C10 and dihedral groups D3–D5 (as the paper states; the released code for the checkpoint we score also samples D2 = Z2×Z2), with the map from group elements to tokens redrawn for every sequence. Their models reach near-perfect accuracy, transfer to held-out order-8 groups, and use identifiable mechanisms: commutative copying, identity recognition and closure-based cancellation. Associative composition is the weakest route, at 60.2% on the sequences that test it. Our forcing classes (@sec:classes) make this an exact per-cell measurement: a loop-forced (O) cell is fixed by identity and cancellation alone, an associativity-forced (G) cell only through associativity. Scored this way, the released In-Context Algebra model is the first real network for which the fingerprint returns a class above R, with Latin- and loop-forced cells at 0.89–0.99 and G cells at 0.53–0.70 at $t = 0$, in line with the authors' ranking of mechanisms (@sec:r-ica). That work evaluates no pretrained language models and does not measure how completion depends on the number of earlier, differently labelled tables; the released model's 16-token vocabulary fits at most one history table, so our compounding contrast cannot be measured on it. Adjacent work studies algebra learned in weights: grokking of finite-dimensional algebras [@notsawo2026grokking], and in-context modular arithmetic that generalises out of distribution only above a threshold number of pretraining tasks [@he2024grok]. "Cayley-table completion" in [@huh2025algebraic; @huh2026cayley] is a different problem, recovering a table by tensor factorisation rather than inferring it in context.

#### Memorising and generalising learners

Raventós et al. [@raventos2023] find a task-diversity threshold in transformers trained on linear regression: below it the model acts as a Bayesian estimator over the discrete set of pretraining tasks, above it as ridge regression. Wurgaft et al. [@wurgaft2025] explain such transitions as a posterior-weighted trade-off between a memorising and a generalising predictor. Our exemplar learner N_iso and abstraction learner A (@sec:learners) are in-context analogues of these predictors, with the "seen tasks" supplied in the prompt under fresh labels. On identical trials both gain about +0.70 over the scramble control on G cells, and only held-out types separate them cleanly: +0.70 for A against +0.03 for N_iso (@sec:r-ideal). Chan et al. [@chan2022] report that transformers trained on controlled stimuli generalise from context mainly through exemplars, whereas pretrained language models do so in a more rule-based way, increasingly with scale. Our design would also separate the two by the shape of compounding (gradual for exemplar matching, a step at $t = 1$ for abstraction), but none of the nine pretrained checkpoints compounds, so the prediction remains untested. If in-context learning is implicit Bayesian inference [@xie2021], a learner that already holds the true environment prior gains nothing from fresh-label histories; our Bayes learner B makes this exact, scoring 1.000 on G cells with and without a history.

#### Recall and induction-style copying

Mechanistic work traces in-context learning to identifiable circuits. Induction heads complete a repeated pattern AB…A with B and coincide with a phase change in in-context ability [@olsson2022], and symbol-abstraction, symbolic-induction and retrieval heads together carry out abstract rule induction in language models [@yang2025symbolic]. Our recall-only learner R corresponds to verbatim, induction-style copying, and the leak control, whose last history table is the target's own labelled table, measures that ability directly. Recall depends on family more than on size: under the leak control, G-cell accuracy is 0.52–0.65 for Qwen3 models up to 1.7B, 0.95 and 0.96 for Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, 0.44 for SmolLM2-1.7B and 0.31–0.37 for OLMo-2, yet every one of these models is class R (@sec:r-llm). In the larger Qwen3 models, then, the absence of compounding is not a failure to read the context; the prerequisite inference is missing.

#### Pretrained models and structure defined in context

Several studies ask whether pretrained models adopt semantics defined only in the prompt. Overriding flipped labels and learning from semantically unrelated labels emerge with scale [@wei2023larger]. Performance on counterfactual task variants, such as arithmetic in unfamiliar bases, degrades relative to the default task [@wu2024reasoning], and language models underperform simple n-gram baselines on randomly sampled formal languages [@gupta2025random]. Analyses across the Pythia suite place in-context learning between memorisation and an independent symbolic algorithm [@niu2025illusion]. Models reorganise their representations to match a graph defined only by random walks in context [@park2024iclr], yet often cannot use such representations downstream [@lepori2026], and representational change within a context suggests that in-context learning is not one process [@hosseini2026]. Our null result fits this picture: up to 8B, no checkpoint infers Latin-forced cells in direct-answer mode, and none of the eight swept over cue density does so even at the densest cue, where one-pass row/column elimination would score 0.92 (post hoc; @sec:r-cal).

#### Cross-episode gains and length-matched controls

Matsutani et al. [@matsutani2026] come closest to compounding: few-shot demonstrations from different worlds, with the same or a different graph topology, improve in-context world modelling across several model families. Their worlds reassign one shared set of words to the graph's nodes, whereas our history tables use fresh words, and we found no length-matched control like our scramble history. Two raw increases from $t = 0$ to $t = 4$ in our data were reliable, and both vanish against the scramble history; for OLMo-2-1B at $n = 6$, t4 − t0 is +0.042 [+0.011, +0.073], but t4 − scramble is +0.020 [−0.008, +0.051]. Across all ten model–precision configurations, t4 − scramble lies between −0.019 and +0.026, with every 95% CI including 0 (@sec:r-llm).

#### Activation-space probes of processing mode

A separate line reads the processing mode from activations. Demonstrations are compressed into a task vector [@hendel2023] or a function vector carried by a few attention heads [@todd2023fv], and the Jacobian lens identifies representations a model is poised to verbalise, which carry intermediate steps of silent reasoning while automatic processing proceeds without them, as in a global workspace [@gurnee2026workspace]. The compute–retrieve (CR) axis [@ramdan2026cr] probes mode directly: a mean-difference direction from 30 retrieval and 30 computation prompts, where "retrieve" means recall of facts stored in weights. That preprint notes that its retrieval prompts are shorter than the computation prompts, leaving prompt complexity as a possible confound. Our leak and base conditions remove it for in-context retrieval: they share format, token count and query position, yet accuracy is 0.53–0.97 in leak and 0.17–0.22 in base. The axis separates its own calibration prompts with AUC 0.96–0.99, but on this contrast it moves significantly in the predicted direction in 0 of 5 Qwen3 models and is significantly reversed in 3 (@sec:r-cr). In an exploratory control, context vocabulary explains 64–87% of the reversal; with vocabulary matched, a retrieval signal of the predicted sign appears in the post-trained model ($d_{z}$ +0.36) and without the chat template ($d_{z}$ +0.33 to +1.67). Whether such representations would carry induced axioms across tables is untested.

#### Usable information and critical sets

Storing past tables verbatim and extracting their axioms yield the same Shannon information about the environment. Predictive V-information [@xu2020usable] instead measures what a computationally bounded observer can use, and computation can create it. This frames our equal-budget comparison: N_iso and A enumerate at most the same number of candidate tables and differ only in what the history makes cheaply usable. For Latin squares, a critical set is a minimal set of entries that determines the square, and the smallest one corresponds to the minimum teaching dimension of the class [@hatami2016]. Our forcing classes ask a cell-level version for one fixed cue: which unrevealed cells it already determines, and under which of the nested classes Latin, loop and group.

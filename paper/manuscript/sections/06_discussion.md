## Discussion {#sec:discussion}

#### What the study establishes

Apart from the CR result (@sec:r-cr), the study establishes four things. First, a measurement, validated on exact learners only, that tells abstraction from exemplar reuse in context: the decision rule recovers all seven preregistered exact learners and the post-hoc count learner C without error, and only held-out transfer cleanly separates abstraction from exemplar matching, also against type-matched baselines (post hoc; @sec:r-ideal); as the two are provably identical at prime $n$, a claim of "learning to learn" an algebraic structure in context needs composite orders and held-out types. Second, an upper bound on history benefit in pretrained language models up to 8B, on this format: none reaches the preregistered Latin-inference threshold in direct-answer mode, and for no model does the 95% upper bound of the gain from group histories beyond the length-matched scramble exceed +0.056 (+0.069 with a post-hoc Bonferroni correction), an order of magnitude below exemplar copying or abstraction (@sec:r-llm; @sec:r-cal). In Qwen3 at 4–8B, which recalls a seen table almost perfectly, the shortfall lies in Latin accuracy (below the preregistered threshold), not in reading the context. Third, a count shortcut that trained networks find instead of the task and that alone passes the preregistered learning-failure threshold, so "learned" should be defined against learner C, not chance (@sec:r-trained). Fourth, a positive control: the In-Context Algebra model [@todd2026ica] is non-R, non-B, with a graded group prior (g(G t0) rises from 0.49 at 10k to 0.65 at 100k training steps; exploratory) that a continuous index captures better than the B-type cut-off (@sec:r-ica; @tab:app-ica-traj).

#### What it does not establish

We make no claim about larger models, other formats or test-time reasoning. The results are tied to one format, unordered lines of single-token common English words used as arbitrary labels: exploratory row-sorting helps at $k = 28$, most in Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base (+0.10 to +0.11) and less (+0.005 to +0.046) elsewhere, including the larger OLMo-2-7B (+0.045), an instruction header does not help (−0.08 to +0.04, mean −0.006), and grids or natural-language framing may lower the onset scale, although the fingerprint itself is format-independent, because the classes depend only on the cue. An exploratory reasoning probe was dominated by truncation and neither shows nor excludes reasoning-enabled Latin inference (@app:probe).

The method is validated on exact learners only. On trained networks the Experiment-3 validation criterion is *not met* (A4 and I4 are R, not A/B-type and N_iso) and the depth claim is not supported, which a post-hoc diagnostic attributes to the training rather than to the fingerprint. The In-Context Algebra model is a positive control for steps 1–3 of the rule only, because its 16-token vocabulary rules out the compounding branch.

Nor does Experiment 6 isolate which part of the training matters. The In-Context Algebra model leaves class R after about 0.64–1.28B training tokens, comparable to our roughly 0.91B, but differs in parameters (about 16× ours), width (1024 vs 256), learning rate, symbol vocabulary (16 vs 64 symbols), loss target (every token vs answers) and context density (groups-only, 200-fact sequences vs sparse cues). Exercising the compounding branch on a trained network needs a model of this kind with enough symbols for $t = 4$ histories (30 at $n = 6$, 40 at $n = 8$), since steps 4 and 5 of the rule use G t4, G scr4 and G held4, and training data in which the count shortcut is uninformative. One data property may matter: the In-Context Algebra generator draws facts with replacement, so, for a guess that ignores the queried cell, symbol counts carry no information about the next answer, whereas our generator writes each table without replacement, which makes the least-revealed symbol the best guess that ignores the cell. Sampling facts with replacement, or reweighting the loss so that least-revealed symbols receive only their uniform share of correct answers, would remove the advantage of this cell-blind shortcut; restricting the loss to cells where the least-revealed symbol is wrong would not, because it rewards the reverse shortcut.

Precision limits the largest models. Qwen3-4B-Base, OLMo-2-7B and Qwen3-8B-Base ran in nf4, whose preregistered control on Qwen3-1.7B-Base failed (G accuracy 0.02–0.07 lower, 44% of per-query answers changed); it created no spurious effect, but with nothing to hide at 1.7B it cannot rule out that nf4 hides a weak effect at 4B, 7B or 8B, and nf4 accuracies should not be compared with bf16 levels. The CPU-offload path used for OLMo-2-7B and Qwen3-8B-Base was checked against standard scoring only on smaller models, Qwen3-4B-Base in nf4 and Qwen3-1.7B-Base in bf16, not on those two models themselves (@app:impl), and the Qwen3-8B-Base main file, the last 216 trials of the Qwen3-4B-Base main file and the first 50 OLMo-2-7B calibration trials were scored with script versions that were not archived (@app:deviations, row 8).

#### Threats to validity

Context length is controlled by a token-matched scramble history for every compounding contrast, and the two raw $t = 0$ to $t = 4$ increases whose CIs exclude 0 (OLMo-2-1B at $n = 6$, Qwen3-0.6B at $n = 8$) are no longer distinguishable from zero against it. Held-out targets are non-abelian only, with abelian histories; the type-matched check leaves every classification and the null unchanged. A held-out arm with non-abelian histories (for example D4 histories with Q8 targets) would also separate exemplar matching from over-generalised commutativity, and remains future work. The main analyses carry no correction for multiple comparisons; a post-hoc Bonferroni correction widens the largest $n = 6$ $t = 4$ minus scramble upper bound from +0.056 to +0.069 and removes OLMo-2-1B's raw increase but not Qwen3-0.6B's (@app:mult).

Freezing the rule and thresholds before any output limits analysis flexibility; the only unplanned change to a decision rule, the $\geq 30$-query guard, excluded a preregistered $k = 16$ follow-up that, once run, leaves both affected models R (@sec:r-cal). Post-hoc analyses are labelled and enter no preregistered classification; learner C, post hoc in Experiment 3, was fixed before Experiment 6 was preregistered and is its preregistered comparator (prediction P4 in @tab:app-ica-pred). The scoring checks, the audits and their corrections, none of which changed a fingerprint classification, are described in @app:impl.

#### Implications

A model's improvement after seeing related examples is weak evidence of abstraction unless the improvement survives a length-matched control, the cells measured are forced only by the structure in question, and the improvement transfers to held-out types; solving more cells as the cue grows is weak evidence of constraint inference unless it beats a global count baseline. These tests are not blind: on a network trained for the task they return a non-R class and an associativity deficit, consistent with its published mechanisms [@todd2026ica]. The results bound experience compounding at the scale, format and inference mode tested here, rather than exclude it, and give a falsifiable target: sparse-cue Latin inference above the count baseline should precede any compounding, and compounding with $w \approx 1$ should follow.

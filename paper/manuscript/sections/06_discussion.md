## Discussion {#sec:discussion}

#### What the study establishes

The first result is a measurement that can tell abstraction from exemplar reuse in context. Exact forcing classes and controlled histories (a length-matched scramble, held-out isomorphism types, a leak control and non-associative loops) give distinct, preregistered signatures for recall, Latin bookkeeping, exemplar matching, abstraction and Bayes-with-prior. The decision rule recovers all seven preregistered exact learners, plus the post-hoc count learner C, with no error (@sec:r-ideal). Only held-out transfer cleanly separates abstraction from exemplar matching, and at prime $n$ the two are provably identical, so a claim of "learning to learn" an algebraic structure in context needs composite orders and held-out types.

The second is a calibrated null for pretrained language models up to 8B. None of the nine checkpoints (ten model–precision configurations) from three families infers in direct-answer mode, and none of the eight swept over cue density does so even at the densest cue, where row/column elimination would score 0.92 (a post-hoc baseline; @sec:r-cal). All eight calibrated models are non-inferential on this format under the calibration rule with the $\geq 30$-query guard, and the two that qualify without it remain R in the preregistered $k = 16$ follow-up ($\mathrm{acc}_L$ 0.18 and 0.22). None compounds. Recall approaches ceiling for Qwen3 at 4–8B (0.95–0.96) but not for OLMo-2 (0.31–0.37) or SmolLM2 (0.44), so in Qwen3 the missing ingredient is the prerequisite inference, not reading the context.

The third is a shortcut that trained networks find instead of the task. A post-hoc diagnostic shows that small transformers trained from scratch on group, Latin and loop diets answer with the symbol least represented so far, a global count statistic that matches five of the six learned networks to within 0.001–0.007 (the 6-layer network exceeds it by 0.014) and accounts for the in-training plateau at $n = 6$: 0.33 for the shortcut, 0.29–0.36 for the networks (@sec:r-trained). The fingerprint correctly gives the networks the class of the exact count learner (R), but the shortcut alone passes the preregistered learning-failure threshold (chance + 0.10). Future preregistrations should define "learned" against the count learner, not chance.

The fourth is a positive control on a real network. The released In-Context Algebra model [@todd2026ica] is classified non-R, non-B: on the base $t = 0$ cells, Latin- and loop-forced cells score 0.89–0.99 and associativity-only cells 0.53–0.70, well above learner C and row/column elimination (@sec:r-ica). Across six released checkpoints (exploratory), its class changes from R to non-R between 5k and 10k steps. The categorical classes miss its graded group prior (g(G t0) rises from 0.49 to 0.65 after the transition), which g(G t0) captures better as a continuous index than the B-type cut-off.

The fifth is a validity limit for activation-space "mode" probes. The compute–retrieve (CR) axis [@ramdan2026cr] separates its calibration prompts with AUC 0.96–0.99, but it does not track retrieval versus inference once format and length are matched: it is significantly reversed in three of five models, and its sign flips with prompt format (@sec:r-cr). An exploratory control attributes 64–87% of the reversed gap to context vocabulary. With vocabulary matched, a retrieval signal of the predicted sign appears in the post-trained model (small, $d_z$ +0.36, comparable to its vocabulary effect) and without the chat template ($d_z$ +0.33 to +1.67). In base models under the chat template, the vocabulary effect is 4–12× the retrievability contrast.

#### What it does not establish

We make no claim about larger models or test-time reasoning. The reasoning probe (Qwen3-1.7B, thinking on) was dominated by truncation: 28 of 30 thinking runs hit the 4,096-token budget, and the 2 that finished were correct (@sec:r-probe). It neither shows nor excludes reasoning-enabled Latin inference, and it tests only whether a model can apply a *stated* rule, not induce one from histories.

Validation on trained networks is only partial. The method-validation criterion of Experiment 3 is *not met*: A4 and I4 are R, not A/B-type and N_iso, and the preregistered depth claim is not supported. A post-hoc diagnostic attributes this to the training rather than to the fingerprint: the networks learned a count shortcut, and the fingerprint gives them the same class (R) as the exact count learner C. The In-Context Algebra model supplies a positive control for steps 1–3 of the rule, but its 16-token vocabulary rules out the compounding branch, so N versus N_iso versus A on a real network remains untested.

Nor does Experiment 6 isolate which part of the training matters. The In-Context Algebra model has about 16× our parameters, but its checkpoints leave class R after about 0.64–1.28B training tokens, comparable to our roughly 0.91B, so the token budget does not explain the difference. Width (1024 vs 256), learning rate, symbol vocabulary (16 vs 64 symbols), loss target (every token vs answers) and context density (groups-only, 200-fact sequences vs sparse cues) also differ. Showing the A signature on a trained network needs a model of this kind with enough vocabulary for $t \geq 2$ histories, or a curriculum that defeats the count shortcut at small scale, such as a loss on cells where the least-revealed symbol is wrong.

The three largest models (Qwen3-4B-Base, OLMo-2-7B, Qwen3-8B-Base) ran in nf4 to fit the 8 GB GPU. The preregistered bf16-versus-nf4 control on Qwen3-1.7B-Base failed its tolerance: nf4 lowered G accuracy by 0.02–0.07 and changed 44% of per-query answers. It created no spurious effect and preserved strong context use, but with nothing to hide at 1.7B it cannot rule out that nf4 hides a weak effect at 4B, 7B or 8B. nf4 accuracies should not be compared directly with bf16 levels.

The results are also tied to one format: unordered lines of single-token common English words used as arbitrary labels. In an exploratory row-sorted variant, sorting the target's facts helps at $k = 28$, about equally in Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base (+0.10 to +0.11) and less (+0.005 to +0.046) in every other model, including the larger OLMo-2-7B (+0.045). An exploratory instruction header does not help (−0.08 to +0.04, mean −0.006). Grids or natural-language framing may lower the onset scale. The fingerprint itself is format-independent, because the classes depend only on the cue.

#### Threats to validity and how each was handled

Context length is controlled by a token-matched scramble history for every compounding contrast; two reliable raw $t = 0$ to $t = 4$ increases (OLMo-2-1B at $n = 6$, Qwen3-0.6B at $n = 8$) disappeared against it. Fresh single-token words per table, a random bijection and a semantic blacklist limit leakage through tokens. The audits recomputed the forcing classes of all 2,800 Experiment 1–2 stimulus trials with independent SAT and enumeration code (0 mismatches), as did the same checker on the 12,000 queries of the 600 later addendum-2d trials.

Batched, offloaded and version-mixed scoring were each checked against sequential scoring. Argmax agreement was 92–98%, with near-ties in bf16; the Qwen3-1.7B-Base primary contrast, re-scored with one code version, gave −0.006 against −0.007; and classes are identical on accuracy and on $p_{\mathrm{gold}}$.

Freezing the rule and thresholds before any output limits analysis flexibility. Experiment 3's allowed adaptations (a 2×-steps retrain of A4, an M=8 rerun of P4) did not trigger: A4 passed the learning rule, and P4 did not generalise. One change did matter. The $\geq 30$-query guard on the $k_L$ rule, added after the first sweep had been seen, excluded the $k = 16$ operating point that the frozen rule triggers for Qwen3-0.6B-Base and Qwen3-1.7B-Base (2 of 4 L queries correct at $k = 6$); run after the final audit, it leaves both R. Apart from the guard, no unplanned change (@app:deviations) altered a stimulus, threshold or decision rule. Post-hoc analyses are labelled and enter no preregistered classification. Learner C, post hoc in Experiment 3, was fixed before Experiment 6 was preregistered and is its preregistered comparator (prediction P4 in @tab:app-ica-pred).

Two adversarial audits (2026-10-07, 2026-10-09) re-derived every Experiment 1–5 number that existed at the time; later verification passes (2026-10-09 to 2026-10-11) re-derived the results produced after them (learner C, the count-shortcut diagnostic, the offload and re-scoring checks, the full Experiment 5b run and the addendum-2d run), checked the revised text, and checked Experiment 6 (2026-10-11). They corrected an inflated copy-bias statistic, an unguarded partial-file value, order-dependent bootstrap seeds, stale ranges and model counts, an unapplied learning-failure rule, an overstated "dense-context Latin inference" reading of the trained networks, and the post-hoc guard that had removed the preregistered $k = 16$ follow-up. None changed a fingerprint classification.

#### Implications

For in-context learning research, "the model improves after seeing related examples" is weak evidence of abstraction unless:

1. the improvement survives a length-matched control;
2. the cells measured are forced only by the structure in question;
3. the improvement transfers to held-out types.

Likewise, "the model solves more cells as the cue grows" is weak evidence of constraint inference unless it beats a global count baseline. The released stimulus generator, exact forcing classes, idealised learners (now including C) and decision rule make these tests routine, and Experiment 6 shows that they are not blind: on a network trained for the task they return a non-R class and an associativity deficit, consistent with its published mechanisms [@todd2026ica].

For experience compounding more broadly, these results place the phenomenon, if it exists for algebraic abstractions, beyond the scale and inference mode tested here. They also give a concrete, falsifiable target: the onset of sparse-cue Latin inference above the count baseline should precede any compounding, and compounding with $w \approx 1$ should follow.

For representation-level claims about "modes" of processing, the CR result is a cautionary case: a direction that separates prompt *types* need not track the *process*. A format- and length-matched behavioural contrast, like leak versus base, is a cheap test that any such probe should pass before it is interpreted.

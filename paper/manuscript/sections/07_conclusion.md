## Conclusion {#sec:conclusion}

We built a compounding fingerprint that tells, for partial Cayley tables, whether in-context improvement reflects abstraction or reuse of the examples; its preregistered decision rule recovers seven exact idealised learners without error, but it is so far validated on exact learners only. On this format, none of nine checkpoints (ten model–precision configurations) from three families, up to 8B (4–8B in nf4, whose quantisation control failed), reaches the preregistered Latin-inference threshold in direct-answer mode or gains detectably from group histories beyond a length-matched scramble (every 95% upper bound at most +0.056); small trained transformers learn a count shortcut; the released In-Context Algebra model is non-R, non-B, inferring Latin- and loop-forced cells almost perfectly but associativity-forced cells only in part; and the compute–retrieve (CR) axis does not track retrieval versus inference on a format- and length-matched (not vocabulary-matched) contrast, where context vocabulary accounts for most of its reversal (exploratory; @app:cr).

Three problems remain open: exercising the compounding branch of the rule on a learned system with enough symbols for $t = 4$ histories, more than the 16-token In-Context Algebra model holds; count-shortcut-aware training, in which "learned" is judged against learner C and the data leave the count shortcut uninformative; and matched-vocabulary tests of activation "mode" probes before their projections are interpreted.
